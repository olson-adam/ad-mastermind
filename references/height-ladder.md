# The Height Ladder + the Lemon Check

Two instruments that together measure what naive generation can't: **is the idea unexpected** (the ladder) and **does the execution carry the traits that tend to travel with engaging advertising** (Lemon). Used in concepting (gate + push-pass) and in critique.

## What the ladder IS and ISN'T

The ladder is a **distinctiveness heuristic**: it measures distance from the category's wallpaper, in Byron Sharp's sense (stand out, avoid category-sameness, be impossible to confuse). It is NOT an effectiveness prediction. Two counterweights always apply:

1. **Field's "Crisis in Creative Effectiveness" (IPA 2019):** the effectiveness multiplier of awarded creativity fell 12→4 — chasing what juries reward has demonstrably decoupled from effect when chased for its own sake. Height is a means against ignorability, not a goal.
2. **The novelty↔fluency tension:** mental availability is built by consistency and easy recognition (Sharp/Romaniuk). A low-awareness brand, or one mid-rebrand, can lose on "new territory every time" — there, the series/asset-system is the mechanism and the novelty ambition gets a ceiling. When this tension is active, NAME it in the deliverable.

## The ladder — 1–10, calibrated for static B2B ads

| Level | Label | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | **Harmful** | Embarrassing, off-brand or misleading — worse than silence. Note: ragebait can earn fame AND live here — outrage is not height |
| 3 | **Invisible** | Wallpaper. Doesn't register on a scrolling thumb |
| 4 | **Competent but ignorable** | Well-made, right message, zero friction — and therefore unseen. *"The riskiest move is to be ignorable"* (Greg Hahn, Mischief). The 4 is every pipeline's most common output and its biggest risk |
| 5–6 | **Fresh craft** | A twist on a familiar device; a creative nods, nobody tells anyone about it. First-instinct ideas land here — and **format-borrowing ceilings here** (wanted-poster, government-warning, inspection-protocol aesthetics all judge as "borrowed device" = 6) |
| 7 | **New territory for the category** | Judgeable test: no ad in the field corpus (or the judge's category knowledge) uses the move, AND a named realistic sharer exists ("who screenshots this, with what comment?"). **The delivery floor: median ≥7** |
| 8 | **New for the market** | Judgeable test: the move breaks an advertising-wide convention (not just the category's) — the judge should struggle to name ANY ad using it — and the point still lands for someone outside the audience |
| 9–10 | **Fame / cultural impact** | Mostly an outcome, honestly: score 9+ only when the mechanism is self-proving in the medium (Marina Prieto-class) or the confession/completion is so total the ad redefines the sender's position in one read |

**Rules:**
- **The first-instinct rule:** the first idea per mechanism/insight is assumed to be level 4–6 and may NOT be delivered without a push-pass. The burden of proof is on the concept.
- **Median ≥7 in the delivery.** Adjust only for explicit market logic (narrow TAM: level 7 = "new territory for that audience"; low awareness/rebrand: the fluency ceiling may lower novelty ambition in favor of series consistency — stated, never silent).
- **The judge question:** *"Would the TARGET BUYER recognize their everyday in this, tell a colleague about it — and be able to say WHOSE ad it was?"* Three parts: recognition, share-worthiness, brand linkage. (Not "would an awarded CD raise an eyebrow" — that's the award-proxy Field showed has decoupled from effect; the CD eye belongs in the craft pass, not as effect judge.)
- **Calibrate against [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md) before scoring** — never against gut alone. Our logged testing found self-scores running roughly 1–1.5 steps kinder than independent judgment — treat the exact size as a heuristic, the direction as settled. Assume your first number is too generous.
- **The artifact calibration:** a concept whose wit lives in a GENUINE category-internal artifact (the drawing title block, the site diary, the inspection form) can be 7–8 even when the description reads like a format joke — but only if the artifact looks *found*, not designed. The height sits in the craft; score such concepts "7 conditional on craft" and write the condition.
- **The register calibration: PLAYFULNESS IS HEIGHT.** A set can be technically 7 (category-new, Lemon-right, authentic) and still fail for lacking energy — dry documentary understatement is ONE register, not the definition of height. Absurd humor, playful visual metaphors, and culture-borrowing with meme energy are equal roads to 7+. A delivery where no concept makes the viewer SMILE is mis-registered regardless of ladder score.
- The level is reported per concept in the deliverable, with a one-line justification + **floor score** (the level if execution turns out mediocre — see [kill-gate.md](kill-gate.md)).

## The Lemon check — video-derived heuristic, extrapolated to static

Source: Orlando Wood, *Lemon* (IPA 2019) — System1 analysis of **TV/video advertising**. Right-brain traits (character, place, happening, dialogue, wit) correlate there with higher emotional response and effectiveness; left-brain traits dominate modern advertising. **Honest label: the evidence base is audiovisual. The translation to static ads is a reasonable heuristic — not proven static effectiveness.** For emotion's role in growth, cite Binet & Field (emotional campaigns build share better long-term; ESOV is the other factor) — not unspecified correlation numbers. For B2B specifically, the LinkedIn B2B Institute/System1 work is the right corpus to lean on.

**Right-brain traits to BUILD IN (count occurrences, 0–6):**

| Trait | In a static ad this means |
|---|---|
| A felt sense of **place** | A real environment (site shed, office at 9pm, meeting room) — not studio/void/flat color field |
| **Characters with agency** | People doing/wanting something — not models posing at the camera |
| **Betweenness** | Implicit relation BETWEEN people in the image (glances, tension, situation) — the unread part is what's felt |
| **Line/dialogue** | Spoken language, a quote, someone saying something to someone — not claim-monologue from the sender |
| **Wordplay/language subversion** | Play where the reader completes the point (the Economist mechanism) |
| **Captured moment + depth** | A scene mid-happening, with foreground/background — not a timeless flat layout |

**Left-brain markers to FLAG (warning at ≥3):** flatness/flat color fields · intrusive text-as-graphic · abstracted body parts (hands holding phone…) · monologue/testimonial claim · adjectives as nouns ("simplicity", "control" as load-bearing elements) · self-absorption (the brand talking about itself) · UI screenshot as hero.

**Usage:**
- In the concept spec, right-brain traits are DESIGN REQUIREMENTS: each concept actively picks the 2–3 traits it carries. A concept with 0 right-brain traits and 3+ left-brain markers is category wallpaper by definition — kill or redo.
- **The monoculture warning:** Lemon is diagnosis, not a generative recipe. Optimize every concept toward the same traits and the whole set gets one voice — the voice-diversity rule in kill-gate.md is the antidote.
- Real exception: pure typographic wit (The Economist) has neither place nor people — but it has wordplay + reader-completion carried by a distinct asset system. Weighted instrument, not a checklist.
- In **critique**, existing ads are scored on both lists — the left-brain hits are often the most pedagogical part of the diagnosis.

## Calibration self-test (run whenever the ladder changes)

1. The category default ("dashboard screenshot + 'Take full control'") = level 3–4 with 4+ left-brain markers — if the ladder passes it, the ladder is broken.
2. The Economist mechanism must NOT die on the Lemon check; if it dies, the check is misapplied.
3. The modern 6-band (see benchmarks.md: Vanta "SOC 2", Brex "catch your interest", Baseten "Own your nines") must score 6, not 7: smart insider wordplay is fresh craft, not new territory.
4. A wanted-poster/warning-label format borrow with nothing owned = 6, however polished.
