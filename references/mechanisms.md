# The Mechanism Library — 12 ways from insight to idea

Every prioritized insight runs through AT LEAST 5 of these mechanisms. A mechanism *transforms an insight into an idea* — it is not a style template. The forced mechanism-switching is what stops a model (and a human) from producing ten variants of the same safe idea.

Working rule: generate fast and ugly — one line per raw concept (`[mechanism] × [insight]: [idea]`). Polish only survivors, after the kill-gate.

**Hierarchy (blind-test evidence, with its exception):** *format borrowing* (wanted-poster, authority-warning, protocol aesthetics) ceilings at 6 — "borrowed device" — while *documentary observations from the buyer's reality* reach 7. The logged exception (kill-gate instrument 1, confirmed by a judged run where a true-crime borrow earned 7): **culture-borrowing whose inner logic IS the point, executed with real energy, is a legitimate road to 7.** Therefore: mechanisms 11 and 12 are first-class and are tried on every prioritized insight; every culture-format idea must answer "what do we own here that doesn't leave with the format?".

**The billboard-as-content principle:** a static B2B ad lives by being screenshotted and shared in the feed, not by its paid reach. Ask per concept: *who shares this, with what comment?* If no realistic sharer can be named, the scroll-stop is all it has.

## ⚠️ CALIBRATION — DELIVERY BAN

All examples in this file are **constructed pattern examples** showing each mechanism's FORM. They are spent the moment they appear here. **Hard rule (kill-gate): a concept that essentially recreates an example from this file = kill, regardless of score.** Same for benchmarks.md: the corpus calibrates the judge; a concept mimicking a corpus entry is by definition ≤6 on the ladder ("new territory" cannot be borrowed).

## 1. Analogy / metaphor from outside the category
**Mechanism:** borrow a structure everyone already understands from another domain and map it onto the insight. The brain rewards solving the connection itself.
**Recipe:** take the insight's core dynamic (chaos→order, hidden→visible, slow→fast) and ask: where in everyday life does the exact same dynamic exist, familiarly?
- *Accounting platform, insight "year-end close = panic":* a marathon runner starting to run at the 41 km sign. "The close doesn't start in March."
- *Cybersecurity, insight "you notice the breach too late":* a house with 14 locks on the door — and an open basement window.
**Fits:** cold/off-market. **Trap:** an analogy that needs industry knowledge to decode is dead. Test on an outsider.

## 2. Juxtaposition / contrast
**Mechanism:** two elements that don't belong in the same frame, or before/after pushed to its edge.
**Recipe:** put the insight's two poles side by side — but replace at least one pole with something unexpected. "Messy screenshot vs clean dashboard" is the category default (= spent). Elevate: what is the *human* contrast?
- *Same person, two frames: 9am in the demo meeting (proud) vs 9pm the same evening (digging for attachments in email).* The contrast is the feeling, not the UI.
- *Industrial component, insight "cheap part, expensive failure":* a $0.40 gasket next to a stopped production line. Price tags on both.
**Fits:** all audiences; strongest for in-market when the contrast is economic. **Trap:** symmetric, expected contrast (the good/bad split everyone does) — mechanism without idea.

## 3. Dramatized buying situation (the moment as mini-scene)
**Mechanism:** recreate the exact moment the need arises — so specifically the audience feels seen/caught. Recognition is B2B's most underused emotion, and it links straight to the memory structure you want to own (situation → brand).
**Recipe:** take a buying-situation scene verbatim from the field/insight work. Show the scene, not the solution. The product is at most the sender.
- *The meeting line as headline:* "Hold on, I have that in a different system." — white text, meeting-room photo, logo. Done.
- *Onboarding:* new consultant's day one: 9 invites to 9 tools, one welcome email: "most of it is in Teams. Or Drive. Ask Anna."
**Fits:** cold + off-market (the heart of demand creation). **Trap:** sneaking product pitch into the scene kills the recognition. The scene is 100% the buyer's world.

## 4. Exaggeration / absurdism
**Mechanism:** drive the insight to logical absurdity. The exaggeration makes the point unmissable AND signals self-distance — itself distinct in a stiff category.
**Recipe:** "if this problem continued for 10 years / existed in another industry / were taken completely seriously — what does it look like?"
- *Insight "every tool has its own login":* a keychain the size of a bowling ball. "Day 1 as project manager."
- *Insight "status reports eat time":* an org chart where one full-time role is titled "Looks for the latest version".
**Fits:** cold audiences, brands that can carry a glint. **Trap:** absurdity without anchoring in real pain = "weird ad"; the exaggeration must be a true feeling × 100.

## 5. Culture borrowing
**Mechanism:** hook into an existing memory structure — film trope, sports moment, meme format, national everyday canon — and the ad borrows a structure that already lives in the head.
**Recipe:** find the cultural reference whose INNER LOGIC matches the insight (not just its surface). Meme formats work when the format's point = the insight's point.
- *Insight "everyone promises 'one platform for everything'":* trailer aesthetics: "FROM THE PRODUCERS OF 'We'll email you the spreadsheet'…"
- *Insight "the migration gets postponed every year":* New Year's resolution format in January: "This year we're actually switching systems."
**Fits:** warm audiences best; cold if the reference is broad. **Traps:** (a) dated memes — older than ~6 months = embarrassing; (b) copyright/trademarks — borrow the FORM, never protected material; (c) references only marketers get.

## 6. Villain
**Mechanism:** give the frustration a face. A common enemy builds kinship between brand and buyer — and the villain can be as ugly as needed without pointing at any customer. The villain is NEVER a competitor — it's status quo: the tool sprawl, the email thread, version hell, "how we've always done it".
**Recipe:** personify or objectify the enemy. Name it. Let it recur (= distinctive device, see mechanism 9).
- *The villain "The Attachment":* portrait as wanted poster: "WANTED: Final_FINAL_v7_NEW(2).pdf. Last seen: someone's desktop."
- *The villain "the cc swamp":* group-photo aesthetics, 14 people: "Everyone who needed to see this email." (all but one greyed out)
**Fits:** all quadrants — one of few mechanisms that works both off-market (emotion) and in-market (problem framing). **Trap:** an abstract enemy ("inefficiency") — the villain must be something you can SEE.

## 7. Insider truth
**Mechanism:** say what everyone in the industry thinks but nobody says publicly. Creates the "finally someone said it" reaction — shareable, screenshotable, and positions the brand as the one that actually gets it.
**Recipe:** hunt the shameful/unsaid in the field quotes. State it straight, no hedging, ideally as a claim in giant typography (the typographic ad without image is a legitimate static format).
- *"Your intranet is a graveyard with a search function."*
- *"Nobody reads the status report. Everyone still asks Anna."*
**Fits:** cold/off-market (fame-building), LinkedIn especially (comment-driving). **Trap:** a truth that insults the BUYER instead of the situation — the edge points at the system failure, never at the person living in it.

## 8. Visual pun / literal interpretation
**Mechanism:** take an idiom, an industry phrase or a word from the insight and show it LITERALLY. Image + words solve each other — the viewer gets the point in two steps, and the reward makes it memorable.
**Recipe:** list phrases from the field work (the quotes!) and the category language. Which becomes an image if you mean it literally?
- *"The knowledge sits in the walls":* a photo of post-its, binders and USB sticks BRICKED INTO a concrete wall.
- *"It fell between the chairs":* two office chairs, a gap, a case file on the floor in a spotlight. Chalk outline around it.
**Fits:** everyone; the low-risk mechanism with height — works even for cautious brands. **Trap:** a pun that needs the copy to work — the image carries 80%.

## 9. Distinctive device / character (the series builder)
**Mechanism:** create a recurring element — a character, an object, a visual world, a format — that accumulates recognition across campaigns. This is the mechanism that builds LONG-TERM mental availability: part 5 of a series compounds in a way one-off ads never do.
**Recipe:** take the strongest villain, character or format idea from the other mechanisms and ask: "does this hold for 10 executions?" A device must be (a) instantly recognizable at thumbnail size, (b) connectable to several buying situations, (c) impossible for a competitor to copy without advertising you.
- *The character "Anna who knows everything"* (the bottleneck personified, with love): a series where every episode is a new question Anna gets.
- *The format "This week's missing file":* wanted poster as recurring format — new filename every time.
**Fits:** brands with ongoing commitment (not one-off campaigns). **Trap:** a device that's just a mascot with no link to insight/situation — recognition builds without associations.

## 10. Demonstration on steroids
**Mechanism:** proof — but dramatized into an IMAGE, not a bullet. For in-market/warm audiences proof is the right message; this mechanism exists so proof doesn't have to mean boring.
**Recipe:** take the strongest number/proof (case, time saved, users) and give it a physical form or unexpected comparison.
- *"14 hours of admin per week" →* a calendar where 2 of 5 workdays are crossed out with "search, wait, remind".
- *Time proof →* an hourglass filled with shredded strips of printed email threads.
**Fits:** in-market + warm (capture). **Trap:** believing the number in big font IS dramatization — the number needs a body.

## 11. Documentary observation (FIRST-CLASS)
**Mechanism:** show the buyer's reality EXACTLY as it looks — a real place, a real moment, a real wall — and let a single line do the reframe. No metaphor, no borrowing: the unedited truth is what nobody in the category dares to show. In our early blind testing this mechanism carried the concepts that independently reached 7 (small n — treat as a strong prior, not a law; energetic culture-borrows have since matched it).
**Recipe:** what already EXISTS in the buyer's world — places, walls, moments, rituals — that becomes an ad just by being pointed at? The reframe sits in the line, not the image. Requirement: the image must read as FOUND, not staged (art direction that cleans it up kills it — put it as a fragile-flag in the spec).
- *The place where the expensive decision actually gets made (the parking lot, the elevator queue) + the price tag.*
- *The real noticeboard/wall/desk drawer + one line naming it as "the system".*
- *The real moment frozen mid-happening (three people, three drawing versions) + the actual line someone said.*
**Fits:** cold×off-market (recognition's royal class); often needs site access — the cheapest authenticity there is. **Trap:** documentary STYLE without documentary CONTENT (stock photo with film grain); and subtlety that doesn't read at thumbnail size — the reframe line must carry at 6 cm.

## 12. Artifact subversion (FIRST-CLASS)
**Mechanism:** take a GENUINE category-internal artifact — the form, the drawing title block, the protocol, the site diary — and let the ad BE the artifact, with a subversion the industry instantly recognizes as its own everyday. Differs from culture borrowing (mechanism 5): the artifact belongs to the CATEGORY, not the culture — a competitor can't borrow it without imitating, and outsiders' formats don't come along.
**Recipe:** inventory the category's documents. Which one already carries an unsaid truth in its structure (the revision table = version chaos; the diary = what nobody wrote down)? Subvert MINIMALLY — the wit sits in one row or one cell; the rest must be pedantically authentic.
- *The form's/table's last row derailing into the truth.*
- *The official document's fields filled in the way they're ACTUALLY filled in (not the way they should be).*
**Fits:** cold×off + warm (the industry shares its own recognition); series-able per artifact. **Traps:** (a) **the craft condition is hard:** if the artifact looks designed instead of found, the concept dies — require a real reference document in the spec (7–8 WITH the condition, 6 without); (b) an artifact the audience doesn't emotionally own (a generic invoice < the drawing title block); (c) requires reading — spec the thumbnail hook (which row survives at 6 cm?).

## First-instinct traps (skip past these, per mechanism)

The first idea per mechanism is almost always the category's cliché of that mechanism — level 4–6. List the obvious, strike it, continue:

| Mechanism | First-instinct trap (generate past this) |
|---|---|
| 1 Analogy | Puzzle, lego, Swiss army knife, conductor/orchestra, hub of the wheel |
| 2 Juxtaposition | Before/after split, messy vs clean, red cross vs green check |
| 3 Dramatized situation | Stressed person at laptop touching forehead (the stock-photo scene) |
| 4 Exaggeration | "Drowning in paper", person buried under document piles |
| 5 Culture borrowing | Last year's biggest meme; the film quote already in every pitch deck |
| 6 Villain | The "chaos" monster as abstract octopus; Excel as the enemy (every SaaS does it) |
| 7 Insider truth | "Meetings that could've been an email" (spent); generic corporate-BS bingo |
| 8 Visual pun | The idiom already in the brief; sports idioms with no category link |
| 9 Device/series | Mascot without insight link; "our color + a shape" as the only device |
| 10 Demonstration | The number in giant font; bar charts; the logo wall |
| 11 Documentary | Stock photo in documentary style; documentary motif that's still been cleaned up nicely |
| 12 Artifact | The post-it and the whiteboard (everyone's artifacts); parody that exaggerates until the artifact stops being authentic |

**The anti-before/after list (mechanism 2):** before/after is ONE of a dozen side-by-side logics — try Expectation vs Reality · Bad vs Worse · Users vs Non-Users · Without vs With · Expensive vs Cheap before accepting a before/after.

**Humor mechanics (mechanisms 4/5/7):** name WHICH humor type the concept uses (surprise/incongruity, recognition comedy, understatement, absurdism, self-irony, parody, wit) — "funny" is not a mechanism.

**THE FUN ROUND is mandatory:** at least one divergence round runs with explicitly playful temperament — absurd exaggeration/personification, playful visual metaphor, slap-and-hug (the sting that resolves into warmth), culture-borrowing with meme energy. Generate these WARM — self-censoring "too silly" is forbidden in the round; the gate decides later. A divergence output where no raw concept makes a human smile is a failed sweep, not a mature one.

## Usage — three rounds

**Round 1 — the mechanism sweep:** per prioritized insight, pick the 5+ mechanisms with the best surface (one-line motivation) — **mechanisms 11 and 12 are ALWAYS tried**. Generate 2–4 raw concepts per mechanism × insight — AFTER listing and striking the first-instinct trap. Tag each: `[mechanism] [insight-id] [audience]`.

**Round 2 — collisions:** each prioritized insight × 4–6 unrelated domains (nature, sports, cooking, aviation, board games, weather…) — borrow the domain's INNER LOGIC, not its props. 1–2 raw concepts per collision that sparks; don't force dead ones.

**Round 3 — off-script:** no mechanism, no list: "What is the bravest TRUE claim or image here? What would we make if the client couldn't say no?" 2–4 raw concepts.

Total: **20–30 raw concepts.** Do NOT self-censor — bad ideas die cheaper in the gate than they're rescued later. The only forbidden idea is one that already exists in the batch, in this file, or in the benchmarks corpus.
