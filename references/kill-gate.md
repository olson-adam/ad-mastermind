# The Kill-Gate — instruments, height floor, portfolio rules

Used in concepting as the culling phase, and in critique as the scoring frame. Read [height-ladder.md](height-ladder.md) FIRST — the ladder and the Lemon check are the gate's sharpest instruments.

**Calibration truth (from our blind testing):** a naive gate discriminates at the bottom but not at the top — gate-killed raw concepts scored 5–6 blind, the same band as several *delivered* concepts. The gate's job is not just killing wallpaper; it must kill **6s**. A smart insider pun or a fresh format borrow is a 6 (see the modern 6-band in [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md)) — nice, but not delivery.

## Adversarial protocol

For every raw concept: argue AGAINST it first. One sentence: "This concept dies because ___." Strong counter-argument → kill immediately, log one line. Default is kill — survival must be earned. Of 20–30 raw concepts, **5–8** survive to the push-pass; more survivors means the gate is too kind.

## The instruments, in order

### 0. Freshness check
Read benchmarks.md's "Last updated" line. Older than 6 months → flag it in the deliverable summary and continue — a warning, not a block.

### 1. The height ladder (1–10)
First cull. **Level ≤4 = kill immediately.** Level 5–6 survives to the push-pass ONLY if the core can take escalation — and the **format-borrow test** runs here: is the concept built on a borrowed culture format (wanted poster, authority warning, protocol aesthetics, auto-reply)? Then the ceiling is 6 until something is OWNED that doesn't leave with the format. TWO exceptions: (a) a genuine category-internal artifact (instrument 4); (b) **culture-borrowing with energy:** when the format's inner logic = the insight's point AND the execution has comic/meme energy, the borrow is a legitimate road to 7 — don't reflex-kill it as "borrowed device". The difference: a tired format worn as a costume = 6; a format whose point IS the joke = can be 7.

### 2. The Lemon check
**0 right-brain traits + ≥3 left-brain markers = kill** (category wallpaper by definition). Exception: pure typographic wit with reader-completion + a distinct asset system. Mind the monoculture warning — Lemon must not homogenize the set (portfolio rule 5).

### 3. The uniqueness gate (the competitor test)
Two steps, both mandatory:
- **The competitor test:** *"Could any competitor sign this concept as-is?"* YES = kill or redo — regardless of height. The concept must be built on something only THIS sender can credibly say/show/own: their customers, their data, their position, their asset system.
- **The logo-off test:** cover the logo — what in the ad still points at the sender? Zero = kill-flag; for a low-awareness brand or one mid-rebrand it is a hard kill rule. Note new asset candidates (device, format, color world, character) per concept so recognition can be built deliberately across a series (Romaniuk's fame×uniqueness logic).

### 4. **The field test (this tool's addition)**
Every surviving concept must cite, from the field analysis (`field-stats.json` + the classified message codes):
- **which wallpaper convention it breaks** — a named phrase/format/code that 2+ advertisers in the field share ("full control", demo-CTA monoculture, dashboard-hero…), and
- **its nearest neighbor in the field** — the existing ad it could most be confused with, and why it can't be.
A concept that breaks no named field convention is not automatically dead — but it must then earn its place purely on height ≥7, and the deliverable must say "in-convention" honestly. A concept confusable with a running competitor ad = kill (the field just proved it's already wallpaper).

### 5. The quick tests
- **"Get it?":** does an outsider get the point in 3 seconds without industry knowledge — or (for insider concepts) does the target buyer get it in 1 second? No on both = kill.
- **"Three executions":** does the idea hold for three different executions? If not, it's a one-off gag, not an idea — park as tactical single or kill. (Device candidates must hold for 10.)
- **The artifact test:** does the wit sit in a genuine category-internal artifact? Then the concept can be 7–8 — but requires the craft condition "looks found, not designed" as a fragile-flag in the spec, plus a real reference document.

### 6. Hygiene scorecard (8 dimensions, 0–5 — internal culling tool, never shown in the client-facing deliverable)

| # | Dimension | Kill rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scroll-stop | ≤1 = kill (unseen smart = unseen) |
| 2 | Emotion (named, felt in the gut) | 0 = kill |
| 3 | Buying-situation link | — (weight by market logic) |
| 4 | Outside-in (buyer's world, not sender's) | ≤1 = kill |
| 5 | Differentiation — against the field's verified conventions AND the batch's other concepts | ≤1 vs a batch sibling = keep the strongest, kill the duplicate |
| 6 | Brand linkage (uniqueness gate deepens this) | 0 = kill at low awareness/rebrand |
| 7 | Boredom dividend (does it make the category less boring?) | doubtful = kill |
| 8 | Static carrying capacity (ONE still, mobile, 80% copy-independent) | ≤1 = kill or park as video concept. Reading-required concepts (artifact/typography) are legitimate exceptions but must spec the thumbnail (what survives at 6 cm?) |

### Hard kill rules outside the scales
- **Example-recreation:** a concept essentially recreating an example from mechanisms.md or an entry in benchmarks.md = kill, regardless of score.
- **Rational quota:** rational capture concepts without emotion only when explicitly planned, tagged `capture` — a quota, never a default.
- **The ragebait rule:** provocation whose point IS the outrage = level 1–2 regardless of attention. Provocation is fine when the hug exists (slap-and-hug) — the slap must be redeemed.

## The push-pass (after the gate, before delivery)

Each survivor is escalated 3 times — this is what moves 5–6 → 7+:
1. **"Turn the volume":** the same idea, twice as far. What happens at 2×? 10×?
2. **"Remove the safety net":** strike the hedge, the explanation, the softener. What remains when the idea stands unprotected?
3. **"The idea behind the idea":** which deeper truth made the idea good? Build a new version directly on that truth.

**Sharpening (from our blind testing):** the push-pass alone isn't enough — self-scores after push still judged 6 independently. After the push: rerun instruments 1+3+4 on the pushed version asking *"is this still a form device, or do we now own something?"*. A pushed 6 is a 6.

Keep the strongest version (which may be the original — but proven against the escalations). Note in the deliverable which push won.

## Portfolio rules (on the survivors as a GROUP)

1. **Mechanism spread:** ≥4 different mechanisms among survivors.
2. **Test-cell logic:** no two concepts share both mechanism and insight.
3. **Height floor:** median ≥7 after the push-pass — by INDEPENDENT calibration (assume self-score is +1–1.5; compare against the modern 6-band before granting a 7).
4. **Voice diversity:** ≥2 genuinely different aesthetic registers among survivors (documentary photographic / typographic wit / graphic system / artifact) — not just different mechanisms. Max 1–2 pure text concepts per set.
5. **Temperature quota:** at least half the delivery carries PLAYFUL energy (absurd humor / playful visual metaphor / slap-and-hug / meme-energy borrowing) — dry understatement is the spice, not the dish. Premium/dignity briefs invert this quota, stated explicitly.
6. **Floor-score spread:** at least half the delivery must be ROBUST (holds its height in mediocre production). A portfolio of only fragile 7s is a portfolio of future 5s.

## Calibration examples (self-test every run)

- "Dashboard screenshot + 'Take full control'": height 3–4, 0 right/4+ left, emotion 0 → **dead three times over.** If it passes, the gate is broken.
- The Economist mechanism must NOT die on the Lemon check.
- A sharp but senderless typography ad must STICK in the uniqueness gate (any competitor could sign it) until tied to something owned.
- The modern 6-band (Vanta, Brex, Baseten) passing as 7 = the ladder is too kind.
